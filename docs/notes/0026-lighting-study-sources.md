# Annotated lighting-design sources

Date consulted: 2026-09-29

Status: reading record, not a list of material merely collected. Tags: #references #research

The scope column says what was examined. “Read” means the linked article or named
sections, not all works by that author. Online manuals may change; this record
identifies the publication/version displayed where available. No paid books or
full concert films were read/watched. Summaries are deliberately short; follow the
original sources for their examples and context.

## Foundations and color practice

### R01 — ETC: Objectives of Lighting Design

[Article](https://blog.etcconnect.com/stage-lighting-design-part-2-objectives-of-lighting-design).
Primary manufacturer teaching material, based on Declan Randall's course content.
Read: objectives and examples. Useful for defining what a scene is supposed to
accomplish. Scope: theatrical introduction; it is not an automatic concert policy.
Used in [composition](0021-composition-and-attention.md).

### R02 — ETC: Controllable Properties of Light

[Article, 14 September 2023](https://blog.etcconnect.com/stage-lighting-design-part-3).
Read: intensity, color, distribution and movement. The discussion includes balancing
attention and the fatigue of excessive changes. Useful vocabulary for separating
our controls. It does not prescribe universal numeric settings for a small rock gig.

### R03 — ETC: Lighting Angles

[Article, 9 November 2023](https://blog.etcconnect.com/stage-lighting-design-part-5).
Read: front, side, back, top and up-light sections. Useful for connecting fixture
position to visibility and shape. Geometric examples are not substitutes for
venue measurements, actual beam coverage or rigging assessment.

### R04 — ETC: Color

[Article, 7 December 2023](https://blog.etcconnect.com/stage-lighting-design-part-6).
Read: additive/subtractive mixing, reflection, complementary colors, filter
selection and dimming. Useful beginner model; real emitter spectra, material
reflectance and calibration need more than ideal RGB examples.

### R05 — Rosco: Guide to Color Filters

[Publisher PDF](https://cn.rosco.com/sites/default/files/content/resource/2022-10/Rosco_Guide_to_Color_Filters_22.pdf).
Read: opening guidance, designer contributions and introductory color discussion;
not an exhaustive study of every gel entry. Primary manufacturer material with
firsthand designer contributions. Gel numbers and application suggestions should
not be mistaken for interchangeable LED RGB values.

### R06 — ETC: LED Fixture Color Calibration and Matching

[Technical support article](https://support.etcconnect.com/ETC/Getting_Started_with_ETC_and_FAQ/LED_Fixture_Color_Calibration_and_Matching).
Read: emitter binning, calibration, gamut, direct mode and comparison procedure.
Strong source for what ETC's own systems do. Do not extend its matching guarantees
to unknown low-cost fixtures or uncalibrated direct-emitter modes.

### R07 — NIST: Solid-state lighting metrology

[Research-program description](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/solid-state-lighting-metrology).
Read: color-quality and measurement discussion. Primary research institution;
useful background on why spectra and object appearance complicate LED evaluation.
This is a program overview, not a report measuring our equipment.

### R08 — W3C: CSS Color Module Level 4

[Version examined: 26 September 2026 Candidate Recommendation Draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/CRD-css-color-4-20260926/).
Read: color-space descriptions, interpolation (§13), related conversion guidance
and gamut discussion. Primary standards work, explicitly a draft. Applies directly
to display color authoring; using it for fixture control requires an additional
device model. Used in [color](0022-color-and-fixtures.md).

### R09 — Björn Ottosson: A perceptual color space for image processing

[Original Oklab article](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/).
Read: motivation, comparisons, conversion model and requirements. Primary author
reference for Oklab. Useful for palette paths and perceptual comparisons; not a
physical lighting simulator or proof of gamut-independent uniformity.

### R10 — Lindborg and Friberg: Colour Association with Music Is Mediated by Emotion

[Full 2015 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4671663/).
Read: abstract, method description and conclusions. Primary experimental research
using a CIE Lab interface and interviews. Its music/color association task is not
a study of autonomous punk/metal stage direction. Use it to question simplistic
mappings, not to turn reported associations into mandatory cue rules.

## Firsthand concert-design practice

These interviews provide testimony by the designers themselves. Their publication
context includes industry and manufacturer promotion. We use the design reasoning,
not equipment endorsements, and do not claim controlled proof of audience response.

### R11 — Rob Koenig: Five Lessons in Light

[Interview/advice, 6 January 2026](https://chauvetprofessional.com/news/rob-koenig-5-lessons-in-light/).
Read: the designer's numbered advice. Useful for artist ownership, the audience's
view and the creative value of constraints. His preferences should inform questions
for our artist rather than silently replace their taste.

### R12 — Rob Koenig and Metallica: I'm With the Band

[Firsthand interview](https://chauvetprofessional.com/news/im-with-the-band-rob-koenig-and-metallica/).
Read: design objectives, artist/audience connection and performer emphasis. Useful
counterexample to an effects-only definition of metal lighting. Stadium resources
and the band's established process cannot be assumed in our small venue.

### R13 — Rob Sinclair: American Utopia on Broadway

[Interview, 13 March 2020](https://www.livedesignonline.com/theatre/burning-down-house-american-utopia-broadway).
Read: collaboration, visual narrative, transitions and blackout choices. Useful
case study of a show developing over time. Explicitly staged and repeated material;
our live system lacks its advance knowledge and choreography.

### R14 — Rob Sinclair: Unfinished Light

[Firsthand interview](https://chauvetprofessional.com/news/rob-sinclair-unfinished-light/).
Read: movement, silhouette, artist collaboration and restricted-color examples.
Useful for studying how small differences carry a look. It demonstrates one
experienced practitioner's choices, not a rule against color or movement.

### R15 — Rob Sinclair: Queen Extravaganza

[Firsthand interview](https://www.livedesignonline.com/business-people-news/rob-sinclair-lighting-queen-extravaganza-tour).
Read: visual goal, progressive revelation, palette and venue adaptation. Useful
for thinking about what to hold in reserve. Its song-specific programming is not
an algorithm for recognizing a chorus from our inputs.

### R16 — Andi Watson: Mitski's The Land

[Charlotte Gunn interview, 27 October 2025](https://www.wallpaper.com/art/music/mitski-the-land-lighting-andi-watson).
Read: artist intention, choreography, sightlines, stillness and film capture.
Useful for making the performer the subject and respecting differing audience
views. Interview read; the film itself was not watched. Linked photographs do not
establish what every seat sees.

## Motion and musical inference

### R17 — ETC Eos: Effects Editor

[Official manual](https://www.etcconnect.com/WebDocs/Controls/EosFamilyOnlineHelp/en/Content/14_Effects/01_Effects_Editor/About_the_Effects_Editor.htm).
Read: cycle, scale, grouping/trail, random modifiers and entry/exit. Footer displayed
Eos Family 3.3.10 Rev A. Useful vocabulary and operational distinctions, not a
requirement to implement a console clone. Seeded behavior can be repeatable even
when its appearance is irregular.

### R18 — MA Lighting: Phasers

[Official quick-start chapter](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.0/HTML/qsg_phasers.html).
Read: introductory steps, speed and phase distinction. URL names 2.0 but the page
header displays Version 1.9; keep that discrepancy visible. Useful conceptual
reference only, not verified current API/programming syntax for a console.

### R19 — Müller / FMP: Novelty-Based Segmentation

[Teaching notebook](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C4/C4S4_NoveltySegmentation.html).
Read: self-similarity, checkerboard novelty and examples. Primary educational
material from music-information-retrieval researchers. A candidate boundary does
not imply a named song section; offline context must not silently enter live claims.

## Automatic lighting research: useful, with limits

### R20 — Zhao et al.: Automatic Stage Lighting Control: Is it a Rule-Driven Process or Generative Task?

[arXiv:2506.01482v1, 2 June 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01482v1).
Read: task representation, method overview, dataset description, human evaluation
and discussion. Primary preprint. The [implementation note](0024-design-to-engine.md)
records the main limitations and the statistical claim we should not overinterpret.
No model reproduction or dataset download was performed.

### R21 — Zhao et al.: Stage Light is Sequence²: Multi-Light Control via Imitation Learning

[arXiv:2605.03660v1, 5 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03660v1).
Read: abstract/introduction, architectural decomposition, hardware appendix and
Appendix H limitations. Primary preprint, not established deployment evidence.
Useful research direction for separating desired appearance from venue allocation.
No dependency or learned model was added to shr-lux.

## Flash-related limits

### R22 — Epilepsy Action: Photosensitive epilepsy

[Guidance, page modified January 2026](https://www.epilepsy.org.uk/info/seizure-triggers/photosensitive-epilepsy).
Read: flashing-light factors, visual field and pattern/color-change discussion.
Specialist public-health guidance. Consulted to avoid calling our short/random
patterns inherently safe, not to derive a medical assessment or venue clearance.

### R23 — W3C WAI: Three Flashes or Below Threshold

[WCAG 2.2 explanatory page](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html).
Read: criterion, intent and threshold/context discussion. Primary web-accessibility
source. Relevant to future screen previews; its scope does not certify live stage
strobes. See [timing and storm note](0023-musical-time-and-motion.md).

## Further study: books not read in this pass

The following are bibliographic leads, not sources for claims that require the
unread book text. No purchase is needed to start the free exercises above.

### B01 — J. Michael Gillette and Michael McNamara, Designing with Light

[Eighth edition, Routledge, 2025](https://www.routledge.com/Designing-with-Light-An-Introduction-to-Stage-Lighting/Gillette-McNamara/p/book/9781032582900).
Publisher search record identifies an introductory stage-lighting text. Direct
page retrieval returned HTTP 403; only publisher-indexed metadata was available.
Suggested role: a systematic foundations course alongside practical exercises.

### B02 — James L. Moody and Paul Dexter, Concert Lighting

[Fourth edition, Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Concert-Lighting-The-Art-and-Business-of-Entertainment-Lighting/Dexter-Moody/p/book/9781138942929).
Publisher-indexed record examined; direct page returned HTTP 403. Suggested role:
concert-specific practice and production constraints. Chapters and claims have
not been reviewed here; confirm the edition/contents when obtaining a copy.

### B03 — Josef Albers, Interaction of Color

[50th anniversary edition, Yale University Press, 2013](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300179354/interaction-of-color/).
Publisher page read, including its description of color studies. Suggested role:
train observation of contextual color effects. This is an art/perception text,
not LED additive-mixing arithmetic or fixture-calibration instructions.

## Deliberate exclusions and open leads

- Generic “red means anger” charts: insufficient basis for an automatic cue policy.
- Forum answers: useful leads, but official manuals were used for effect semantics.
- Product marketing: not evidence of our performance, color accuracy or fixture suitability.
- Tour photographs alone: not a measured record of timing, brightness or audience experience.
- Full console manuals, complete books, concert films and model experiments: not represented as completed study.

The next depth step is to obtain a foundational book, annotate actual performances
with their music, and test the specific alternatives in the [audition plan](0025-design-audition-lab.md).
Further reading should answer a question exposed by those trials, rather than
become a growing folder of unexamined links.
